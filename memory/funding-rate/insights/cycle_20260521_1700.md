## Walk-forward — "collect the next funding" gross harvest

**Setup.** For each shortening event:
- `pre_rate_last` = funding rate of last period BEFORE the interval changed (under old interval)
- `post_n1` = funding rate of first period AFTER change (under new interval)
- `post_mean4` = mean rate of first 4 periods after change
- Hypothetical strategy = take the position direction OPPOSITE the existing funding direction
  (i.e. SHORT-perp if pre>0 so longs pay you; LONG-perp if pre<0 so shorts pay you).
- "PnL per dollar" = realised funding payment direction × magnitude. **GROSS — does not include
  underlying price move, slippage, fees, or hedge.**

## Sign-direction asymmetry

Of the 213 shortening events: **162 had pre-rate < 0** (short squeeze, shorts paying longs through
the nose) vs **50 had pre-rate > 0**. Exchanges shorten the interval much more often when the
funding rate is already deeply negative — which makes mechanical sense: shorts trapped > longs trapped
because shorts pay forever and lose to upside drift simultaneously, vs longs who at least get a
spot tailwind on the dump that drove rate negative.

For the extreme-magnitude filter (|pre|≥0.5%): **97 of 98 events have pre<0**. The "trapped shorts"
regime is the dominant signature of shortenings.

## Honest caveats

1. **GROSS funding only. NO PRICE RISK ACCOUNTED FOR.** Holding a single-leg perp 1h or 4h is exposed
   to underlying volatility, often >1-5% over 4h on small-caps. A naïve unhedged trade could
   lose 3% spot while harvesting 2.1% gross funding = net −0.9%.
2. **Implementation requires a hedge.** The textbook hedge: SHORT-perp + LONG-spot when funding<0
   (because then perp-short receives the funding and spot-long captures any upside revert). This
   is the H30 basis-trade setup. Cost: borrow rate on spot leg, transfer fees if cross-venue.
3. **R6 (rejected naive funding harvest, −$304/315 trades) is NOT contradicted.** R6 fired on any
   rate >2% without an event-trigger. Here the trigger is the EXCHANGE'S OFFICIAL interval change,
   which is a regime confirmation, not a transient spike. Conditional set is different.
4. **Liquidity unknown.** 122 symbols include some thin small-caps. Spot leg availability per
   exchange must be checked. BTC/ETH likely never see shortenings.
5. **Detection latency.** Need an API poller (H29) running at ≤60s cadence to catch interval-change
   notifications. Most exchange endpoints update once per minute; some announce the change in
   advance via separate notifications board (Bybit, OKX do — Binance silent on it).
6. **Survivorship bias check.** The 122 symbols are whatever the data-pipeline scraped over 180d
   — if there were symbols that delisted before they could have an interval-change event, they
   are absent. Doesn't materially affect direction but could inflate frequency estimates.
7. **Walk-forward is honest but tight.** 64-event test sample. Re-test once more months ingested.
