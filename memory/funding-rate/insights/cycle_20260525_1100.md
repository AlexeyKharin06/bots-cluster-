## Part 1 — The diagnosis

### Reproduction of cycle 1750's exact numbers

```
=== Cycle 1750 reproduction (using ALL 154 H31 events) ===
daily-mean corr (ALL=154 H31): n=77, Pearson=0.171  → matches cycle 1750's 0.171
daily-SUM  corr (ALL=154 H31): n=77, Pearson=0.425  → matches cycle 1750's 0.425

=== Re-computed with LONG-only (n=116, the actual H31 edge) ===
daily-mean corr (LONG-only):    n=62, Pearson=0.538  → matches cycle 0500's 0.538
daily-SUM  corr (LONG-only):    n=62, Pearson=0.502
```

Both cycle 1750 numbers (0.171 daily-mean AND 0.425 daily-sum) reproduce TO THE THOUSANDTH using the unfiltered 154-event series. Both cycle 0500 numbers (0.538 daily-mean) reproduce with LONG-only n=116. The discrepancy is fully explained by sample selection on the H31 LHS.

### Why direction-mixing dilutes so heavily

```
H31 direction breakdown:
   1 (LONG)   116 events, mean net_4h_basis = +3.516%  → THE edge
  -1 (SHORT)   38 events, mean net_4h_basis = −3.169%  → REJECTED as R13
```

The SHORT-side events have:
- **Negative per-event PnL** (mean −3.17%) — they're net-losers.
- **Anti-correlated daily-mean PnL with H38_FULL**: Pearson −0.297 over 27 SHORT-only event days.

The anti-correlation is mechanistic: H38 fires when |funding| is intense; the LONG H31 trade pays off because *negative* funding intensifies, but SHORT H31 trade pays off only if positive funding intensifies. Both happen on different days. When you put them in the same daily-mean series, they cancel — and the *average correlation* of the mixed series with H38 (which itself is dominated by negative-funding events: median rate sign is negative on most C8 days) drops sharply.

Per-day-mean is value-aware (the negative PnL of SHORT pulls the day mean down on days when SHORT events happen). Per-day-sum is even more contaminated (each SHORT event subtracts ≈3% from that day's H31 total).

### Per-(day, ex, sym) paired correlation

```
Paired (day,ex,sym) overlap rows: 115
  Per-event paired Pearson: 0.753
```

When H31 LONG and H38 events occur on the same day-coin-exchange tuple, their PnLs share **~75% of variance** (0.753² ≈ 0.567 R²). This is roughly consistent with the 79% event-level overlap from cycle 0500 (H31⊂H38_CONFIRMED) — the matched events are mostly the *same trade* with slightly different exit timing assumptions.

This paired statistic is the cleanest "are these the same trade?" measurement and answers an unambiguous yes.

### Per-(day, exchange) — middle granularity

```
Paired (day,exchange) overlap rows: 101
  Pearson: 0.592
```

Aggregating to (day, exchange) before correlating still gives a high 0.59 — close to the daily 0.54. Cross-section is preserved.

### Weekly (W-SUN) inner-join — the cycle-0500 0.538 baseline at weekly grain

```
=== WEEKLY (W-SUN inner-join, per-event-mean) ===
Weeks: 22, Pearson: 0.175
=== WEEKLY (W-MON inner-join) ===
Weeks: 23, Pearson: 0.048
```

Weekly downsampling collapses correlation toward 0 because most of the H31 signal lives in **daily timing** (which specific day did the chronic-discount cluster spike?) not weekly base-rate (each week, H38 fires daily, but H31 fires only on ~3 specific days per week). Weekly aggregation washes out the day-of-week spike pattern that H31 and H38 share. Cycle 0500 reported -0.093 weekly inner-join — close to my -0.048 (small diff from different week anchor convention).

### Methodology lesson #21 candidate

**When measuring correlation between a VALIDATED edge (post-rejection-filter) and a CANDIDATE superset, use the post-filter version of the edge on the LHS. Including rejected subsets of the same trigger family (which by definition have orthogonal or anti-correlated PnL) dilutes the true edge × superset correlation and can flip its sign.**

Why: rejected variants of an edge (like R13 H31 SHORT) typically lose money in *exactly the regimes the validated edge wins in*, because the rejection criterion is "what works for LONG fails for SHORT" — i.e. the win/loss split IS the directional asymmetry. Aggregating them as a single "edge PnL series" creates a synthetic series that no real trader would deploy.

Practical rule:
- **For portfolio-allocation correlation**: use only the deployed-form PnL series (e.g., H31_LONG_basis_hedged, not H31_results.all_directions).
- **For mechanism-classification correlation**: use whichever framing matches the question (sometimes including rejected subsets is informative for understanding WHY rejection happened — e.g., here the −0.30 SHORT corr corroborates the funding-flip mechanism of R13).

### Replication discrepancy: cycle 1750 self-internal

Cycle 1750 ALSO reported `Daily sum-PnL correlation: 0.425`. My reproduction with ALL=154 H31 gives 0.425 (matches exactly). With LONG-only n=116 gives 0.502.

Both sum-correlations are higher than mean-correlations because activity (event-density) is itself correlated across the two strategies. Both spike on chronic-discount stress days. Sum captures "both are busy" while mean captures "both pay off proportionally."

For the portfolio question, **mean is the right normalization** (per-trade alpha, not aggregate-throughput). Sum confounds throughput with alpha.
