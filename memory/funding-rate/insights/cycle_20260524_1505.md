- Implication: **adaptive routing of WHICH mechanism to trade based on regime adds no Sharpe.** It might affect capital deployment (more opportunities in some regimes), but mean-per-trade is unchanged.

## METHODOLOGY #19 CANDIDATE

**Title**: Separate `frequency(mech | regime)` from `mean_PnL(mech | regime)` when validating regime-adaptive hypotheses.

**Statement**: A practitioner-derived hypothesis of the form "in regime X, mechanism Y is more profitable" needs two independent validations:
1. **Frequency check**: does mechanism Y trigger more events in regime X? (Practitioner posts + opportunity counters.)
2. **Conditional-PnL check**: when mechanism Y *does* trigger in regime X, is mean per-event PnL higher than in other regimes? (Backtest with full event-set, not just practitioner-mentioned subset.)

If only (1) holds and not (2), the right response is **capital deployment planning** (allocate more in regime X because more opportunities), NOT **mechanism switching** (route to Y in regime X).

**Generalization**: A class-wide bias in "regime-adaptive" strategies. Most adaptive trading systems conflate these two; the conflation is profitable to discover early because it preserves Sharpe by not over-fitting to small per-regime sample artifacts.

**Application to our backlog**:
- H_adapt_1..3 — REJECT as edge-selectors (PnL is regime-agnostic).
- H_adapt_5 — re-frame as opportunity-rate predictor for capital planning, not edge selector.
- Future: when proposing new adaptive hypothesis, *first* require a passing PnL-conditional-on-regime test on the candidate mechanism's full event sample.

Filed for graduation to canon if a second adaptive hypothesis falls to the same pattern.
