### The leakage discovery

Pause before deploying. Note: `rugger_blacklist.json` was last modified 2026-05-19T16:59 — AFTER many of our trades. The wallet entries include a `tokens` field listing tokens each wallet has rugged. **Are those tokens overlapping our closed_trades?**

Symbol-overlap check:
- 2260 unique token symbols in blacklist
- 420 unique token symbols in our closed_trades
- 121 overlap (28.8% of our trade symbols are in blacklist token-lists)

Decontamination test — split blacklist into:
- **CLEAN** (3260 wallets with NO listed-token overlap with our trades)
- **DIRTY** (468 wallets WITH overlap — meaning we have traded a token they later got tagged as rugging)

TEST window results:
- **PC ∈ CLEAN**: n=141, **avg=-68.1%, WR=0%, rug=52.5%, big=0%** ← INDISTINGUISHABLE FROM BASELINE
- **PC ∈ DIRTY**: n=195, avg=+17%, **WR=82.6%, rug=7.7%, big=4.1%** ← ALL THE EDGE
- PC ∈ ANY: n=336, avg=-18.7%, WR=47.9%, rug=26.5%, big=2.4%
- PC ∈ NONE: n=595, avg=-67.5%, WR=0%, rug=53.1%, big=0%

**100% of the apparent edge lives in DIRTY wallets** — the wallets we recognize because the blacklist labels them via tokens we already traded.

### Mechanism of the leakage

The blacklist is built (presumably on D:\OnChain\ classifier) by scanning chains for wallets that rug-pull LP. Tokens get rug-tagged AFTER the rug happens. A wallet ends up in the blacklist with its rugged tokens listed.

If our trade on token T was a winner: we sniped the pump phase, exited near the top with TP/trail, and the rugger then rugged T after our exit. The blacklist then tags this wallet with T listed. When we look back, we see "wallet was a known rugger → trade was profitable" — but that knowledge was only available IN HINDSIGHT after we already exited.

CLEAN wallets are LP ruggers we have no overlap with — either we never traded their tokens, or our trades on their tokens were rugs (we got out at -90%, the token was already in their rug history before our entry, but the wallet wasn't yet flagged). The CLEAN cohort behaves identically to baseline because the "known rugger" label by itself carries no prospective signal — it's only the wallet-token co-occurrence that does, and that co-occurrence is hindsight.

### Lesson

External classifier DBs that update over time can encode information about the test window's outcomes. **For any classifier used as a feature: must verify the classifier wasn't constructed with hindsight from the test data.** The decontamination split (in vs out of overlap with test tokens) is a quick smoke test.

Time-localization check from last cycle (1AR1WDTonbum top1_owner — single-day artifact) addressed a different leakage form. This is a new form: not concentration in time, but information injection from test→classifier→features.

Status: **REJECT H_RUG_PC, H_RUG_LP, H_RUG_ANY family.**

## Time-correct alternative: in-trade history fields

The trades already carry frozen-at-entry-time snapshots: `entry_signal.lp_hist` and `entry_signal.cr_hist` (each with {pumped_alive, pumped_died, pumped_rugged, rug_no_pump, total_good, total_rugs, total, known}).

Because these are snapshots taken at entry, they CANNOT leak future info. They're the proper way to feed wallet-history into the model.

### lp_hist (confirms last cycle's H_LP_WHITELIST)
- `lp_hist.pumped_alive ≥ 1` on TEST: n=204, avg=-41.5%, WR=11.3%, rug=31.9%, big=3.9%
- vs baseline TEST: n=931, avg=-49.9%, WR=17.3%, rug=43.5%, big=0.9%
- Δ: avg +8pt, rug -12pt, big +3pt. Modest, persistent.

### cr_hist (NEW finding — REVERSE signal vs lp_hist)
Coverage:
- `cr_hist.pumped_alive>0`: only 284 / 4652 trades (6.1%)
- `cr_hist.total_good>0`: 290 (6.2%)
- `cr_hist.total_rugs>0`: 0 (never populated — interesting; classifier doesn't backfill creator-rug history?)

Effect:
- `cr_hist.pumped_alive ≥ 1` TRAIN: n=132, avg=-49.8%, WR=21.2%, rug=42.4%
- `cr_hist.pumped_alive ≥ 1` VAL: n=66, avg=-59.7%, WR=0%, rug=47%
- `cr_hist.pumped_alive ≥ 1` TEST: n=86, avg=-71.2%, **WR=0%**, rug=66.3%

**Creators with prior pumped tokens are WORSE than baseline.** Counter-intuitive but consistent.

Interpretation: lp_provider and pool_creator are different roles.
- **LP provider** with prior winners = professional liquidity service (e.g. repeat LP wallet that backs serious deployments). Their next pool is more likely to be a real launch.
- **Pool creator** with prior winners = serial pump-scammer who deployed a token that pumped before. Their pumped-then-died/rugged track record means they know how to attract retail; their NEXT launch is built to repeat the pattern — pump then rug.

### Combined NEG filter
- TEST excluding `cr_hist.pumped_alive ≥ 1`: n=845, avg=-47.7% (only +2pt vs baseline -49.9%) — too small effect to matter on its own.
- TEST `lp_hist.pumped_alive≥1 AND cr_hist.pumped_alive=0`: n=204 (lp_hist hit set doesn't overlap cr_hist hits — confirms different wallet roles).

## What I considered and discarded this cycle

1. **H_LP_RUG_BLACKLIST as feature** — same family as H_RUG_PC. Same leakage. Rejected.
2. **H_WALLET_HISTORY_DB_LP filter** — wallet_history_db.json's lp role set (2035 wallets). LP_provider ∈ this set matched 4493 of 4652 trades — basically all of them. Not selective. Rejected.
3. **H_CR_HIST_POSITIVE (cr_hist.pumped_alive ≥ 1 as positive filter)** — actively reverse signal. The negative version (exclude these) is too small for standalone use (~6% of trades).
4. **H_RUG_PC × early-TP** — capped-PnL simulation: cap +30% on TEST PC-rug trades → avgCapped=-26% (vs -18.7% uncapped). Capping makes it worse because the rare big winners DO contribute. So even if exit logic were "snipe and quick-TP", the PC-rug signal wouldn't outperform — and the signal itself is leakage anyway.
